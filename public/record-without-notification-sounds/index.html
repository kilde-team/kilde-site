Keep notification chimes out of the recording

Per-app audio capture — sounds from other apps stay out

A message arrives mid-recording and its chime lands right on top of the sentence you needed. kilde can record one window with only that app's sound: the audio follows the app you capture, so notification chimes and everything else playing on your Mac stay out of the file — no muting discipline required, no virtual audio driver.

Record one app's picture and sound

  1. Pick the window. In the menu bar app, choose the window you want. From the CLI, name it: kilde rec --window "Keynote" demo.mov (a title or bundle-ID partial match). Whole screen is fine too — --display or --region — and --exclude-app keeps a specific app's picture and sound out of a full-screen capture.
  2. The audio follows the app. When a single window is captured, its audio is scoped to that app — a message ping, music from another app, or a calendar chime never reaches the file.
  3. Stop safely. Ctrl+C or the stop button always finalizes the file.

The flip side, stated honestly: sounds the captured app itself plays are exactly what you asked for, so they do come in — if the meeting app plays its own join chime, that is in the take. Audio-only recordings (--no-video) capture system audio as a whole, and there the per-app scoping does not apply.

The part audio scoping cannot cover: what is on screen

A chime you have kept out of the file can still flash across the picture. If the screen is being recorded and banners are visible, turn on a Focus mode before you start — macOS then holds banners and their sounds back for the duration. Sound-wise kilde already has you covered; this closes the visual half.

Why this needs a tool like kilde

The built-in screen recorder captures the microphone only, and a whole-desktop capture takes every sound your Mac plays, chimes included. kilde sits on ScreenCaptureKit, macOS's native capture framework, which is what makes per-app audio possible at all — and it costs nothing: free, open source, MIT-licensed.
